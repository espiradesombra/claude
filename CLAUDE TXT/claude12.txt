Dear Editorial Board,

We are pleased to submit the manuscript titled "Quijote + ZYPYZAPE: An Asymmetric Inertia Buffer for Wind Turbine Stabilisation" for consideration as a Technical Note in Renewable Energy.

The increasing penetration of variable renewables reduces synchronous inertia in power grids, causing faster and deeper frequency deviations. Existing synthetic inertia solutions rely on power electronics or external storage. This work proposes a purely mechanical alternative: a dense fluid (Fe+oil suspension, ρ = 3386 kg/m³) displacing radially inside each blade, modulating the rotor's moment of inertia in real time.

Key contributions:

1. Quijote inertia buffer — a hydraulic mass sliding within the blade channel (Ø5 cm, 50 m stroke), generating an oscillating buffer power of 12–16 kW per turbine. A check-valve circuit decouples fluid flow from mass motion, enabling partial energy recovery (128–226 W at η = 85%).

2. ZYPYZAPE asynchronous pitch — each blade receives an independent pitch angle proportional to its angular position, breaking the conservative-field symmetry and improving P_grid by +1.4–1.5% over the baseline NREL 5 MW turbine.

3. Positional speed limit |ṙ_q| ≤ ω·r_q — an original physical constraint introduced by the author: the radial fluid velocity cannot exceed the local blade tip speed. This limit is positional (varies from 6.6 m/s at r_hub to 74 m/s at r_tip) and is always respected at the design point (DR_MAX = 0.5 m/s).

4. Asymmetric energy dissipation — the negative net energy (E_net < 0) is explained as an inherent consequence of the centrifugal force asymmetry, not a modelling error, providing a physically honest characterisation of the buffer.

All results are benchmarked against the NREL 5 MW reference turbine (Jonkman et al., 2009) using a realistic Ornstein–Uhlenbeck wind profile including wind lulls. The complete Python simulation (Gemell v9.4) is available for reproducibility.

This work addresses a critical challenge for grid operators managing high renewable penetration and offers a low-cost, maintenance-friendly mechanical solution that can be retrofitted to existing turbines. We believe it will be of strong interest to the readership of Renewable Energy.
